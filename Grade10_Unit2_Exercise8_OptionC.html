GIVING FOOD WASTE A SECOND CHANCE

Every day, large amounts of food are thrown away by households, restaurants, and supermarkets. Some of this food is no longer safe to eat, but much of it is wasted simply because people buy more than they need or refuse to use food that does not look perfect. Besides wasting money, throwing away edible food also has consequences for the environment.

When food is produced, resources such as water, land, and energy are required. [I] Transporting food from farms to shops also consumes fuel. [II] Therefore, when food is thrown away, the resources used to produce and transport it are wasted as well. [III] Food waste that ends up in landfills can also produce methane, a greenhouse gas that contributes to global warming. [IV] As a result, reducing food waste can benefit the environment in more ways than people may realize.

Fortunately, households can take several practical steps to deal with the problem. Planning meals before shopping can prevent people from buying unnecessary food. Checking what is already in the refrigerator is also useful. In addition, food that looks less attractive should not automatically be thrown away. A strangely shaped carrot, for example, can still be perfectly good to eat. Leftovers can also be stored safely and used for another meal instead of being immediately discarded.

However, reducing food waste does not depend only on individual households. Supermarkets can offer imperfect-looking fruit and vegetables at lower prices, while restaurants can provide customers with suitable containers for unfinished meals. If businesses and consumers change their habits together, a considerable amount of food could be kept out of landfills.
