📡 Reading Transmission

[I] Teenagers often want more independence as they grow older. [II] They may want to choose their own clothes, spend more time with friends, or make decisions about their studies. [III] This desire for freedom is a natural part of growing up. [IV] However, it can sometimes cause disagreements between teenagers and their parents. One common source of conflict is the difference in expectations. Teenagers may believe that they are old enough to make their own decisions, while parents may feel that their children still need guidance. For example, parents might set rules about going out, using smartphones, or completing homework. Teenagers sometimes consider these rules too strict, but parents often create them because they are concerned about their children's safety and responsibilities. Another challenge involves communication. During an argument, teenagers may become frustrated when they feel that their parents do not understand them. Parents, on the other hand, may become upset if their children refuse to listen to their advice. If both sides react angrily, a small disagreement can quickly become a serious conflict. Therefore, learning to stay calm and listen carefully can prevent many unnecessary arguments. Fortunately, disagreements do not have to damage family relationships. Parents can explain the reasons behind their rules instead of simply telling teenagers what they must do. Teenagers, meanwhile, can show that they deserve greater independence by behaving responsibly. When parents see that their children can make sensible decisions, they may become more willing to give them freedom. In this way, trust and open communication can help families find a balance between parental guidance and teenage independence.
