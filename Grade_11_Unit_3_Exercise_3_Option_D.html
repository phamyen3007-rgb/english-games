For decades, Eastport Harbour was mainly associated with warehouses, heavy industry, and cargo ships. As manufacturing gradually moved elsewhere, however, much of the waterfront became abandoned. Rather than allowing the area to continue declining, the local government introduced an (1) ______ redevelopment programme aimed at transforming it into a vibrant and sustainable urban district. A major priority has been to preserve the harbour's historical character while adapting old buildings to modern needs. Several former warehouses have been (2) ______ into offices, cultural centres, and affordable apartments. By reusing existing structures instead of demolishing them, planners have reduced construction waste while maintaining the area's distinctive identity. Transport has also played a central role in the transformation. New tram lines and cycle routes have improved the district's (3) ______ to the rest of the city, making it easier for residents to reach workplaces and essential services. At the same time, restrictions on private vehicles have contributed (4) ______ a noticeable reduction in both congestion and air pollution. This economic growth, however, has resulted in rapidly rising property prices. These features can absorb excess rainwater and provide shade during increasingly frequent heatwaves. Although the transformation has received considerable international attention, planners acknowledge that physical improvements alone cannot guarantee long-term success. Residents must have a meaningful (7) ______ in decisions affecting their neighbourhood. If environmental, economic, and social priorities are carefully integrated, the project could become a model of urban (8) ______ for other former industrial districts.
