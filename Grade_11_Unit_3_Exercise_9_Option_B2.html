📖 Reading passage

As climate change increases the risk of extreme weather, future cities will need to become more resilient. Urban planners are already considering how buildings, transport systems and public spaces can continue functioning during heatwaves, floods and other emergencies. One important strategy is to design infrastructure that can adapt to changing conditions, (1) ________.

Green infrastructure can also help cities deal with environmental pressure. Trees, parks and rooftop gardens can lower urban temperatures, while wetlands can absorb excess rainwater during heavy storms. (2) ________. For this reason, many cities are now including natural features in their long-term development plans.

Energy systems are another area of concern. Cities that depend on a single source of power may be vulnerable when supply is interrupted. Some planners therefore recommend combining solar, wind and other renewable sources with energy-storage technology. (3) ________. This can make the electricity supply more stable and reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

Water management will also become increasingly important. In densely populated areas, demand for clean water may rise while supplies become less predictable. Cities can respond by repairing leaking pipes, collecting rainwater and reusing treated wastewater. (4) ________.

Finally, resilience is not only about technology or infrastructure. Communities must also be prepared to respond when emergencies occur. Local authorities can provide clear information, organise emergency training and ensure that vulnerable residents receive support. (5) ________.
