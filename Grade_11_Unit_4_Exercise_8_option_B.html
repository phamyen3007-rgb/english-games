LEARNING ACROSS BORDERS IN ASEAN

Exercise 8: Read the passage and choose the best answer to each question.

Regional youth cooperation does not always require students to travel abroad. Increasingly, schools in different ASEAN countries organise joint projects in which students communicate online, investigate a shared issue and produce something together. A project might involve comparing local environmental problems, interviewing community members or creating a digital exhibition about traditional practices. Although participants remain in their own countries, they are still exposed to viewpoints that may differ considerably from their own.

The educational value of these projects lies partly in the way students must negotiate meaning. When a group includes members with different levels of English and different communication habits, misunderstandings are likely to occur. Students may need to rephrase an idea, ask for clarification or check whether everyone has interpreted a message in the same way. Such difficulties can initially slow the group down, but dealing with them successfully can make participants more flexible and attentive communicators.

Joint projects can also challenge assumptions. Students sometimes begin an exchange with simplified ideas about another country based on social media, films or limited personal experience. Direct cooperation provides a more complicated picture. A student may discover, for instance, that classmates in another country have very different traditions but similar concerns about exams, future careers or environmental problems. This does not mean that cultural differences become unimportant. Rather, students learn that similarity and difference can exist at the same time.

However, simply placing students in an international group does not guarantee meaningful learning. Teachers need to design tasks that require genuine interaction instead of allowing group members to divide the work and complete separate sections independently. Reflection is equally important. When students are encouraged to consider why a misunderstanding occurred or how their opinions changed, they are more likely to recognise what they have learned from the experience.

Well-designed regional projects can therefore extend students' understanding beyond facts about individual ASEAN countries. They can help young people become more comfortable with uncertainty, more willing to reconsider assumptions and better prepared to cooperate with people whose experiences differ from their own.
