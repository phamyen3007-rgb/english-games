📖 Reading passage

Future cities are often associated with artificial intelligence, automated transport and highly efficient buildings. However, creating a truly smart city involves more than simply introducing new technology. Urban planners must consider whether new systems are affordable, environmentally responsible, and (1) ________. A technological solution may appear impressive, but it will have little value if ordinary residents cannot use it easily.

Some cities are willing to test innovative ideas on a small scale before introducing them more widely. This approach allows planners to identify problems and make improvements without placing the whole city at risk. (2) ________. By contrast, projects that are introduced too quickly may create unexpected difficulties and lose public support.

Another important issue is accessibility. Digital services can make it easier to pay bills, use public transport or contact local authorities. (3) ________. Elderly residents, people with disabilities and those without reliable internet access may otherwise struggle to benefit from these services. For this reason, traditional forms of assistance should not disappear completely.

Public participation is equally important. Residents are more likely to support new urban projects when they understand their purpose and have opportunities to express their opinions. (4) ________. Authorities therefore need to communicate clearly and respond seriously to public concerns.

Ultimately, the success of a future city should not be measured only by the number of advanced technologies it uses. A city can be considered truly smart when technology improves daily life while also protecting the environment and (5) ________.
