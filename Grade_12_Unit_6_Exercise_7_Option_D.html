AI AND THE CHANGING ROLE OF STUDENTS

Artificial intelligence has provided students with unprecedented access to academic support. With a few instructions, learners can receive explanations, generate ideas, and obtain feedback almost immediately. Such tools can be particularly beneficial when students need additional practice outside the classroom. However, easy access to assistance may also change the way learners respond to academic difficulty. Instead of struggling with a challenging problem long enough to understand it, some students may turn to AI as soon as they encounter an obstacle. This habit could gradually weaken their willingness to persevere when answers are not immediately available.

The issue becomes more complicated when students use AI-generated material without evaluating it carefully. AI systems can produce convincing responses, but those responses are not necessarily reliable. They may contain inaccurate information, overlook important context, or present uncertain claims with considerable confidence. Students therefore need to remain skeptical rather than automatically accepting whatever an AI tool produces. Comparing information from different sources, questioning unsupported claims, and explaining why an answer is reasonable are all important parts of responsible AI use. These practices can help students become active participants in learning rather than passive recipients of information.

This does not mean that AI has no place in education. On the contrary, when used purposefully, it can expose students to alternative explanations, provide personalised practice, and help them identify weaknesses in their understanding. The important issue is how responsibility is divided between the technology and the learner. If students use AI to extend their own reasoning, it can be a valuable educational resource. If they routinely allow it to make decisions and complete intellectual work on their behalf, however, the convenience it offers may compromise the very skills education is intended to develop.
