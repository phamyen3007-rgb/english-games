READING PASSAGE

As societies continue to change, differences between generations have become increasingly noticeable. Each generation grows up under different social, economic, and technological conditions, which can shape the way people view family, education, work, and personal freedom. As a result, disagreements between teenagers and their parents are not unusual. Such differences are commonly described as the generation gap. One major source of disagreement is technology. Teenagers have grown up surrounded by smartphones, social networking platforms, and instant access to information, so they often adapt quickly to new digital tools. In contrast, some older adults remain cautious about technological change and prefer more conventional forms of communication. Parents may worry that excessive screen time reduces real-life interaction, while teenagers may feel that adults underestimate the benefits technology brings to their daily lives. Different expectations about independence can also create tension. Many teenagers want greater freedom to make decisions about their studies, friendships, and future careers. Their parents, however, may believe that young people still require guidance because they lack sufficient life experience. Although such disagreements can be frustrating, they do not necessarily indicate a poor family relationship. In many cases, both sides simply want what they believe is best, but approach the situation from different perspectives. Fortunately, generational differences can be valuable when families deal with them constructively. Teenagers can introduce their parents to new ideas and technologies, whereas adults can offer practical knowledge gained through experience. Instead of attempting to eliminate every disagreement, family members should learn to express their opinions respectfully and consider perspectives different from their own. By doing so, differences can become opportunities for mutual understanding rather than sources of lasting conflict.
