THE NEIGHBOURHOOD REPAIR FUND

Every autumn, residents of Brookfield see bright blue repair stands appear outside the public library. Together with posters inviting people to bring damaged household objects, these stands mark the return of the Neighbourhood Repair Fund’s annual campaign. Rather than encouraging people to replace everything they own, the campaign helps families repair essential items they cannot afford to replace. For many residents, its arrival has become a familiar sign that the town’s autumn community festival is approaching.

During the six-week campaign, a local newsletter describes the hardship faced by selected households. One report may feature a parent whose broken cooker makes preparing meals difficult; another may describe an older resident who cannot safely use a damaged wheelchair. Before publishing anything, volunteers obtain permission and remove unnecessary personal details. The aim is to explain why support is needed without making the people receiving it feel uncomfortable. Readers can contribute money, spare parts or professional repair skills.

The idea began in 2018 when a bicycle mechanic discovered that a schoolboy was regularly walking several kilometres to school because his bicycle was broken. After repairing it free of charge, she realised that other families might face similar problems. She invited several tradespeople to organise a small repair day. When more residents arrived than the volunteers could assist, the organisers created a fund to pay for replacement parts and arrange repairs throughout the year.

Since then, the fund has expanded beyond bicycles to include basic appliances and mobility equipment. Its supporters range from secondary-school students to retired engineers and small business owners. A local business association pays the campaign’s publicity and payment-processing expenses, allowing all financial donations to cover repair materials and necessary specialist services. Although the fund cannot accept every request, priority is given to repairs that improve safety or help residents attend school, work or medical appointments.
