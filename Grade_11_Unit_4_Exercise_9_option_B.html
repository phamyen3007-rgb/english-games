PREPARING ASEAN YOUTH FOR A CHANGING REGION

Exercise 9: Read the passage and choose the best answer to each question.

Young people in Southeast Asia are entering a world in which technological, environmental and economic changes are occurring rapidly. New digital tools are transforming how people study and work, while climate-related challenges are affecting communities across national borders. In such circumstances, preparing young people for the future involves more than giving them subject knowledge. They also need the ability to adapt, cooperate and make responsible decisions when situations change.

Regional cooperation can contribute to this preparation. When students from different ASEAN countries work on a shared project, they encounter perspectives shaped by different educational systems, communities and experiences. Reaching an agreement may therefore require participants to explain their reasoning rather than simply state an opinion. Although this process can take time, it encourages students to consider why others may view the same problem differently. In this sense, disagreement can become a learning opportunity rather than simply an obstacle.

Another important ability is dealing with uncertainty. Students are often trained to look for a single correct answer, but many regional challenges have no simple solution. A proposal that benefits one community, for example, may be too expensive or impractical in another. Young people therefore need to examine evidence, consider possible consequences and be prepared to revise their ideas when new information becomes available. This flexibility does not mean changing one's opinion without reason; it means recognising when an earlier decision needs to be reconsidered.

However, developing these abilities requires more than attending an international event. A programme may bring students from several countries together without creating much genuine cooperation. If participants simply listen to speeches or remain mainly with friends from their own schools, opportunities for meaningful learning can be limited. Activities are more effective when students must depend on one another to complete a task, make decisions together and reflect on the difficulties they experienced.

Preparing young people for the region's future is therefore not simply a matter of predicting which jobs or technologies will become important. It also involves developing habits of thought and cooperation that allow them to respond intelligently to circumstances that cannot yet be fully predicted.
